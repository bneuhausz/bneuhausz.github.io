Hatos

A die in your pocket — and it never rolls off the table.

A fast, uncluttered dice roller for board games, RPG sessions and every moment you would rather let chance decide. Tap or shake, roll one die or thirty, build dice out of your own words, emoji or colours, and settle ki kezd? in one throw. Hungarian and English, no account, nothing uploaded. Pro removes the ads and puts a die on your home screen.

Coming soon to Google Play
Privacy Policy • Terms of Use

One die, or a whole handful

Tap the button or shake the phone — the dice tumble, rattle and settle on the felt. Roll one die or thirty, mixed however you like: two six-sided and one twenty-sided in the same throw. Every result goes into a log that survives a restart.

The modes a table actually needs

Coin for heads or tails. Yes / no when all you need is a decision. Who starts? — type the names, the highest roll goes first and a tie is re-rolled by itself. Draw lots — type the options and let the app pick. And poker dice: five dice, three rolls, hold what you like, with the hand named for you.

Dice you make yourself

Build a die out of your own words, emoji or colours — anything from two sides to twenty-four. Rock · Paper · Scissors, a die of everyone's name, a die of what to cook. A plain numbered die can have any side count too: d3, d7 and d30 all roll.

Simple by default, RPG when you want it

It opens as One die · Two dice · Coin, with no notation in sight. Turn on RPG mode and the grammar appears: 4d6kh3, 2d20kh1 for advantage, d%, a +3 modifier, keep-highest and keep-lowest rules — plus a statistics tab showing what has actually been coming up.

Five looks, every one of them free

Blue felt, green felt, midnight, a wooden table and paper. They repaint the table, the built-in dice and the home-screen widget, and they all work in dark mode. Dice you made yourself keep the colours you gave them.

Offline, with no account

No registration, no login, no cloud. Rolling, the log, your own dice, the quick modes and the widget all work in airplane mode; the only network traffic is ads and crash reports. Uninstalling takes everything with it, because it was only ever on your phone.

Why it exists

Everybody has lost a die under the sofa five minutes into a board game, and everybody has argued about who goes first. The phone is already on the table — but a dice app is usually either a single six-sided die with nothing around it, or a wall of ads around a physics engine nobody asked for. Hatos starts from what a table actually does with dice: roll a handful, flip a coin, pick a name, draw lots, and settle it.

That is also why the Hungarian words are the ones on the buttons — ki kezd?, sorsolás, kockapóker are how the thing is asked for in the room, and the English build translates every one of them rather than leaving a word nobody can read. And it is why making your own die is free rather than a paid extra: a die that says Anna · Bence · Csenge · Dávid is the point of a dice app on a phone, not a bonus feature.

Free, with one optional purchase

The app is free and shows a banner ad served by Google AdMob at the bottom of the screen — never a full-screen one, never a rewarded one — with a consent prompt where the GDPR requires it. Hatos Pro is a single one-time purchase: no ads, a home-screen widget that rolls when you tap it, and photo dice faces. No subscription, and nothing else is behind it — the quick modes, custom dice, RPG mode, the log, the statistics and all five skins stay free for everyone. What the app does and does not collect is spelled out in the Privacy Policy.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hatos free?

Yes. The app is free and shows a single banner ad at the bottom of the screen — no full-screen ads and no rewarded videos, ever. A one-time Hatos Pro purchase removes the banner and unlocks two extras. Everything else, including all five skins, every quick mode, custom dice and RPG mode, is free for everyone. There is no subscription.

What does Hatos Pro include?

Three things: no ads; a home-screen widget that rolls when you tap it, without opening the app; and photo dice faces, so your own pictures can be the sides of a die. One payment, no subscription, restorable on a new phone.

Can the widget roll without opening the app?

Yes — tap the tile on your home screen and it rolls there, with the app closed. Note that the widget rolls on tap only: shake-to-roll needs the app open, because a home-screen widget has no process of its own to watch the accelerometer.

Can I make my own dice?

Yes, and it is free. A custom die can have anywhere from two to twenty-four sides, and each side can be your own text, an emoji or a colour. With Pro a side can also be a photo from your gallery. Your dice sit alongside the built-in ones and can be mixed into any pool.

Does it handle RPG notation?

Yes. Turn on RPG mode in the settings and you get dice notation, modifiers and keep rules: 4d6kh3 for ability scores, 2d20kh1 for advantage, d% for percentile, +3 for a flat bonus. A statistics tab shows the distribution of what you have rolled so far. The app starts in simple mode, so none of that is in the way if you do not want it.

Is this a gambling app?

No. Hatos rolls dice — there is no wager, no stake and no prize, and nothing is won or lost. It is a random-number tool, and the app carries a line in its own settings screen saying exactly that. Poker dice is the game of holding and re-rolling; no money and no chips are involved anywhere in the app.

Are the rolls actually random?

They come from the platform's pseudo-random number generator, the same one behind the shuffle in any other app on your phone. Every result is decided before the dice start moving — the tumbling is a picture of a roll that has already happened, not the thing that determines it.

What languages does it speak?

Hungarian and English — the whole interface, including the quick modes and the dice notation. It follows your phone's language by default, and you can pick the other one in the settings at any time.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Rolling, the log, your own dice, the quick modes, the statistics and the widget all work offline. A connection is only used to serve ads in the free version, to send crash reports, and by Google Play for a purchase.

What data does the app collect?

The app itself collects none — no account, no analytics, nothing uploaded. Your rolls, your saved pools, your custom dice and any photos you use as dice faces never leave the phone. Two things do leave it: crash reports, and the data Google needs to serve ads (with a consent prompt in the EEA). The privacy policy has the full details.

What platforms is it on?

Hatos is an Android app, coming soon to Google Play. Other platforms may follow later.
